# Detail

The **Detail** operators work at the smallest and largest scales of the print:

- **Sharpness** and **Grain reduction** at the scale of a few pixels;
- **Bloom** at the scale of a glow;
- **Look strength**, which scales all finishing, is in this section of the panel but described in Chapter 6.

## Sharpness

**Sharpness** is an unsharp mask on the print's lightness, at the output's scale:

$$
Y' = Y + \mathrm{K.SHARP}\cdot\tfrac{s}{100}\,\big(Y - G_{0.7} * Y\big), \qquad \mathrm{K.SHARP} = 1.2 .
$$

$G_{0.7}$ is a 3×3 binomial blur, close to a Gaussian of σ = 0.7 pixels of the rendered image. The gain on any pixel is held between 0.5 and 2, so the strongest settings cannot ring into black or white. The change in lightness is applied to all three colour channels in proportion, so edges sharpen without coloured fringes.

This is output sharpening, for the screen or the printer. It is not the capture sharpening of the design (a Richardson–Lucy deconvolution of the scanner's blur in linear light, §7.6), which the Studio does not have yet. Output sharpening depends on the output's size, so judge it at 100 % on the final file.

- **Range:** 0 to 100, default 0.
- **Use:** 20 to 40 for screen; more for a matte paper, which softens fine detail.

> **Try it.** [Sharpness 50](st:set?sharpness=50) at 100 % zoom. [Off](st:set?sharpness=0).

## Grain reduction

**Grain reduction** smooths each dye layer with an edge-preserving (bilateral) filter. The strength is scaled to the grain expected at the pixel's density:

$$
\mathbf c' = \mathbf c + r\,\big(\mathrm{bilateral}(\mathbf c) - \mathbf c\big) .
$$

$r$ follows the slider, scaled by `K.GRAINRED` = 1.0. A bilateral filter averages a pixel with neighbours of similar value and ignores neighbours across an edge. Grain is a wiggle much smaller than real edges, so it is averaged away while edges stay.

It acts on the dye amounts, where grain is formed, layer by layer (§7.6, item 3). Colour speckle is removed with the grain, before the print's contrast can magnify it.

This is a simple operator, not the noise-spectrum-matched reduction of the design document (4.5), which is not built yet. Strong settings smooth fine texture along with the grain.

- **Range:** 0 to 100, default 0.
- **Use:** 20 to 50 for fast films and underexposed frames. Combine with Grain (Chapter 5) to replace a scan's grain with a finer, more even grain.

> **Try it.** [Grain reduction 60](st:set?grainReduction=60), viewed at 100 %, then [replace the grain with a fine one](st:set?grain=30&grainSize=0.7). [Undo both](st:set?grainReduction=0&grain=0&grainSize=1).

## Bloom

**Bloom** is a soft glow around bright areas, like a diffusion filter on the camera lens or under the enlarger lens. It takes the light of the bright areas, blurs it widely, and adds it back to the print:

$$
XYZ \leftarrow XYZ + \mathrm{K.BLOOM}\cdot\tfrac{b}{100}\;G_\sigma * \big(XYZ\cdot w(Y)\big), \qquad w(Y) = \mathrm{smoothstep}(0.55,\,0.95,\,Y), \qquad \mathrm{K.BLOOM} = 0.6 .
$$

The weight $w$ is a soft threshold: nothing below $Y = 0.55$ glows, and everything above $0.95$ glows fully. $G_\sigma$ is a Gaussian whose $\sigma$ is 3.5 % of the frame's long side.

The glow is thresholded in the low-resolution global pass, blurred at half that size, then added at full resolution (Chapter 11). It carries the colour of the light that makes it.

Bloom and Halation (Chapter 5) both glow, but they are different:

| | Halation | Bloom |
|---|---|---|
| where it happens | in the film, in the red layer's exposure | outside the model, in the print's light |
| colour | red-orange | the colour of the bright area |
| kind | macro | operator |

A diffusion filter could be modelled as a scatter kernel in the enlarger, which would make Bloom a macro. The Studio does not model it that way yet.

- **Range:** 0 to 100, default 0.
- **Use:** 10 to 25 for a gentle glow on night scenes and backlight. More becomes a deliberate effect.

> **Try it.** [Bloom 40](st:set?bloom=40), then compare with [Halation 40 instead](st:set?bloom=0&halation=40). [Off](st:set?halation=0).
