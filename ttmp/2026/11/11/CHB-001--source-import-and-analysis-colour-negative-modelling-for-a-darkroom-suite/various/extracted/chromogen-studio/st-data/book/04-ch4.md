# Crossover and split colour

Printer lights shift all three paper exposures by fixed amounts. A shift can remove a cast that is the same at every density. It cannot remove a cast that changes with density: magenta shadows under neutral highlights, or green highlights over neutral shadows. Printers called this **crossover**, because the three layers' curves cross instead of running parallel. The two colour pads, **Shadow colour** and **Highlight colour**, are the Studio's answer. They are macros, and they are free.

## Where crossover comes from

A negative crosses over when its three layers do not have the same contrast, or when something between the layers changes with density:

- **Mixed light.** Shadows lit by blue sky and highlights lit by the sun give different casts at different densities.
- **Processing errors.** A wrong developer temperature or time changes one layer's gamma more than another's.
- **Expired or badly stored film.** Fog raises one layer's toe and flattens its contrast.
- **Fading.** Dyes fade in proportion to their amount, so the cast grows with density (design document, 4.11).
- **The scanner's limits.** An unmeasured LED or a clipped channel bends one channel's response.

In each case the error in one layer grows with exposure. To first order, it is an offset plus a slope.

## The two pads

Each pad is a two-dimensional control: its horizontal axis is warmth, its vertical axis is tint. Both use the same directions as the Warmth and Tint sliders (Chapter 2), scaled by `K.SPLIT` = 0.5. A pad at (100, 0) is half of Warmth +100, applied at the pad's anchor.

The anchors sit in paper exposure, 0.4 log H either side of each layer's grey point $g_k$ (`K.SPLIT_SPAN` = 0.4). Shadows have *more* paper exposure, so their anchor is above grey:

$$
x_s = g + 0.4, \qquad x_h = g - 0.4 .
$$

With $U_s$ and $U_h$ the light offsets of the two pads, the change applied at exposure $x$ is a straight line between them:

$$
\Delta x(x) = U_s + \frac{x - x_s}{x_h - x_s}\,\big(U_h - U_s\big) .
$$

## An offset and a slope per layer

Write the line out for one layer. At the grey point the change is the mean of the two pads, and the slope is their difference over the span:

$$
\Delta x_k = \underbrace{\tfrac12\,(U_{s,k} + U_{h,k})}_{\text{offset}} + \underbrace{\frac{U_{s,k} - U_{h,k}}{0.8}}_{s_k}\,(x_k - g_k) .
$$

The offset is a printer light. The slope multiplies the layer's exposure about grey by $1 + s_k$, which is exactly what a paper whose layers differ slightly in contrast would do. So the pads are a macro over plausible materials: a paper with three unequal gammas, as an over- or under-developed paper would have, and as darkroom printers sometimes paired with a crossed negative. Only per-layer offset and slope are allowed. Anything curvier, such as a correction that bends in the midtones, would be a new operator, and the pads do not do it.

Because the line continues past the anchors, a large difference between the pads keeps growing into the deepest shadows and the brightest highlights. That is right for crossover, which also keeps growing, but it means large settings should be judged at both ends of the scale.

The pads act on the paper exposure before **Contrast** (Chapter 3). A layer's final contrast is therefore $\gamma\,(1 + s_k)$.

## Using the pads

**To correct crossover:**

1. Balance the midtones with Auto colour, Warmth and Tint, using a neutral near mid-grey.
2. Find a neutral in the shadows (a dark grey coat, a shadowed wall). Move the **Shadow colour** pad until it is neutral.
3. Find a neutral in the highlights (a white wall, a cloud). Move the **Highlight colour** pad until it is neutral.
4. Recheck the midtones. Equal and opposite pad settings do not move the grey point, so they should still be neutral.

**For split toning** the same pads work the other way: warm highlights and cool shadows is the classic, the look of a print toned in two baths.

- **Range:** −100 to +100 on each axis of each pad, default (0, 0).
- **Use:** 5 to 30 for crossover; 20 to 60 for visible toning.

> **Try it.** Make [cool shadows and warm highlights](st:set?shadowColour=-40,0&highlightColour=40,0), the split-tone look. Then try the opposite, [warm shadows and cool highlights](st:set?shadowColour=40,0&highlightColour=-40,0), which reads as late-afternoon light. [Reset the pads](st:set?shadowColour=0,0&highlightColour=0,0) when done.

> **Try it.** Simulate a typical crossover fault, [magenta shadows](st:set?shadowColour=0,40), and correct it with [the opposite move in the shadow pad](st:set?shadowColour=0,0). The midtones never moved.

> **Exercise.** A negative's red layer has a gamma 10 % lower than its green and blue layers. What slopes $s_k$ cancel it, up to a change of density? The pads move only along warmth and tint, whose layer components sum to zero, so look for slopes of the form $(\tfrac23, -\tfrac13, -\tfrac13)\,s$. Find $s$, then check that the offset you need at grey is zero.
