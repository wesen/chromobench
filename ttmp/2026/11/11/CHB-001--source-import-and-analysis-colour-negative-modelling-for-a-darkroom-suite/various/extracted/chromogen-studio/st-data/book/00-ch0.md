# Preface

Chromogen Studio turns a scan of a colour negative into a print. This book explains every control in it: what the control does in print terms, the formula behind it, where in the pipeline it acts, and why it is there. It is the companion to *Colour Negatives from First Principles*. References such as (§10.3) point to that book.

## What the Studio is

A scan of a negative is a measurement, not a picture (§8.6). The Studio does three things with it:

1. It recovers the dyes the film formed.
2. It prints those dyes on a virtual colour paper with three printer lights, as a lab printer would (Chapter 10 of the textbook).
3. It finishes the print with operators that the darkroom never had.

The first two are physics, and the third is computation. The controls are arranged to make that line visible:

- **Print** and **Paper and film** hold the *macros*. Each one sets a parameter the physical model already has: a printer light, a paper, a flash, a film. Whatever you do with them, the result is a print that a real chain of film and paper could have made.
- **Tone**, **Colour** and **Detail** hold the *operators*. These are image operations outside the model: an edge-aware contrast mask, colour moves in a perceptual space, sharpening, bloom. One control, **Look strength**, scales all of them together and never touches the physical print.

The split matters in use. If a frame looks wrong, fix it with the macros first. Exposure, colour and the paper usually get most of the way. Reach for the operators when the print is right but you want more than a print can give.

## How to read this book

- Chapter 1 sets out the pipeline, the units (printer points) and the two families of controls.
- Chapters 2 to 5 cover the macros: lights, paper, split colour and the film's character.
- Chapters 6 to 8 cover the operators: tone, colour and detail.
- Chapter 9 is about working a whole roll.
- Chapter 10 describes how the Studio computes all this on the GPU, and what is not built yet.
- Chapter 11 is about speed: lookup tables, shaders, workers and WebGPU compute, and how to make image algorithms like these fast in general.
- Chapter 12 is a reference table of every control.

Each control gets the same treatment:

- what it does to the print;
- its formula, with the constants named as they are in the Studio's source (`K.MASK`, `K.ZONE` and so on);
- the quantity it acts on;
- whether it is a macro, an operator or a hybrid, and why;
- a sensible range;
- at least one **Try it** link.

## Try-it links

Links in this book drive the Studio.

- A link like [warm the print by 30](st:set?warmth=30) sets that control on the current frame and leaves the others as they are.
- [Reset everything](st:reset) puts every control back to its default. [Reset the finishing only](st:reset?only=finishing) keeps your print and drops the operators.
- [Show the negative](st:show?view=negative) and [show the print](st:show?view=print) switch the view. [Split before/after](st:show?view=split) compares with the snapshot.
- [Load the real Gold 200 sample](st:sample?name=gold200) loads the frame the examples assume.

> **Try it.** Load the sample, then try [a print one stop brighter and warmer](st:set?exposure=1&warmth=25). Then [reset](st:reset) and read on.

Most examples use the built-in sample. It is a real Kodak Gold 200 frame scanned with a Fujifilm X-T5 under three LEDs, the scan studied in Appendix E. The numbers quoted are illustrative unless a section of the textbook is given.
